# Rules, legal and ethical constraints for publishing AI-avatar and cloned-voice videos (state as of 23 Oct 2026)

Reports what sources say; not legal advice. Many sources are secondary (law firms, blogs). Items marked UNVERIFIED need a check against the official page. Several official pages (TikTok, X, Meta, LinkedIn help centers; EU Official Journal) could not be fetched directly in this session.

## 1. Disclosure and labeling rules by platform

### Takeaway
YouTube is the clearest: disclosure is required for realistic content that makes a real person appear to say or do something they did not, but cloning your own voice and AI scripts are explicitly exempt. Meta requires a label for photorealistic video or realistic audio. TikTok has an "AI-generated" label (creator-applied, also auto-applied). LinkedIn relies mainly on C2PA metadata. X: nothing found. An avatar presenter that looks like a real human is the grey area on every platform; the safe habit is to label it.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube "altered or synthetic content" disclosure: required for realistic content generated or meaningfully altered with AI, e.g. makes a real person appear to say or do something they didn't, alters footage of a real event/place, creates a realistic scene that didn't happen, or AI music as the main focus of the video — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- YouTube exemptions: non-realistic content (fantasy, fully animated), minor aesthetic edits, production help (AI outlines, scripts, thumbnails, titles, captions), repair/enhancement (upscaling, voice/audio repair), **cloning your own voice for voiceovers or dubs**, gameplay footage — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- YouTube's page does not specifically address AI avatars/digital likeness of a presenter (fetch summary; the general "realistic person saying/doing something they didn't" test is the likely relevant one) — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- Label placement: on the video player for photorealistic AI content or AI music; in the expanded description for non-music AI audio or animated/non-photorealistic content. YouTube may auto-label content made with its GenAI tools, with C2PA metadata, or detected as AI — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- Consistent non-disclosure can lead to a label added by YouTube (not removable by the creator), content removal or YPP suspension. Disclosure alone does not limit audience or monetization eligibility — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491); [Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/apps-software/youtube-ai-synthetic-content-disclosure)
- Meta (Instagram/Facebook): Help Center says Meta may require an "AI info" label when content has photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio that was digitally created/modified, including with AI; not required for images (images can still be auto-labeled); penalties may apply for missing labels — [Meta Help Center](https://www.meta.com/en-gb/help/artificial-intelligence/1783222608822690/); background: [Axios, Feb 2024](https://www.axios.com/2024/02/06/meta-ai-labeling-fix-oversight-board)
- TikTok: creator-applied "AI-generated" label for content "completely generated or significantly edited by AI" (Sept 2023); synthetic media rules from March 2023 require realistic deepfakes to be clearly labeled; synthetic media showing real private figures is barred; deepfakes of public figures not allowed for political or commercial endorsements — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/new-labels-for-disclosing-ai-generated-content?lang=en); [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2023/03/21/tiktoks-new-guidelines-say-ai-generated-media-must-be-disclosed)
- TikTok 2026 summaries (secondary blogs) say visible labels are expected when AI generates or significantly alters people, voices or realistic scenes — [EchoTik](https://www.echotik.live/blog/tiktok-ai-content-labeling-rules-2026/); [Darkroom](https://www.darkroomagency.com/observatory/what-brands-need-to-know-about-tiktok-new-rules-2026)
- LinkedIn: labels in-stream gen-AI visuals via C2PA metadata (small tag top right); LinkedIn said it cannot identify all AI content; for video, C2PA largely depends on tools attaching credentials — [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/396225/); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/11/26/linkedin-content-credentials-labeling-challenges-c2pa)
- Vendor terms also push disclosure: HeyGen prohibits presenting AI outputs as entirely human-made in a misleading way and requires disclosing AI origin when law requires — [HeyGen Terms](https://www.heygen.com/terms)

### Inferences
- Own cloned voice on YouTube: no disclosure required per the exemption. Own-likeness avatar that looks photoreal: arguably "realistic person", and the page is silent; labeling costs little (disclosure does not reduce reach or monetization per YouTube).
- Cross-platform, EU rules (section 4) make a visible AI label on a realistic avatar the lowest-risk default.

### Gaps
- Exact current TikTok support-page wording and whether label is mandatory vs "encouraged" (blogs differ; official page fetch failed).
- X (Twitter) synthetic and manipulated media policy: no source found.
- Whether Meta's label rule has a distinct treatment for obviously stylized avatars: not found.

## 2. Monetization risk

### Takeaway
YouTube's "inauthentic content" (renamed from "repetitious content", 15 July 2025) targets mass-produced/templated videos, not AI use as such. A faceless-avatar channel is at risk if videos look templated and repetitive; the policy also bars monetizing AI personas posing as human experts on sensitive topics. Meta has a parallel unoriginal-content rule. TikTok Creator Rewards treatment of AI content is contradictory in sources.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube renamed "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content" on 15 July 2025, covering "repetitive or mass-produced" content; not allowed: image slideshows, templated storylines, scrolling text with minimal/no narrative, and characters in the same situation repeatedly; allowed: shared intros/outros if main content differs, series with distinct storylines — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- Policy text: AI content made with "generic or unoriginal templates" that gives impression of mass production is not allowed; using AI to visualize a character/narrative you invented, edit scripts or make background visuals is allowed; automated/template output "must still demonstrate your creative vision" — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- Channels with AI-generated personas presenting as human experts on health, legal, financial or political topics cannot monetize (examples: AI "doctor" diagnoses, AI hosts giving financial guidance) — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- Reused content: repurposing without significant commentary/substantive modification is ineligible even with permission; monetization can be removed channel-wide — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- July 2026 clarified YPP guidance (16 July 2026) lists three inauthentic categories: generic/template videos, unsatisfying or off-putting content, AI personas on sensitive topics; framed as clarification, not new policy; YouTube exec reportedly said AI "enables stuff that's kind of content farming" — [Android Headlines](https://www.androidheadlines.com/2026/07/youtube-monetization-rules-ai-slop-inauthentic-content.html); [AIR Media-Tech timeline](https://air.io/en/monetization/youtube-monetization-policy-changes-2026-a-complete-dated-timeline) (secondary)
- Enforcement detail (UNVERIFIED, single low-quality site): human review after algorithmic flag, 30 days to fix — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/blog/youtube-updates-july-2026)
- Also reported (UNVERIFIED, uncorroborated): YPP overhaul announced 10 Aug 2026, effective 1 Feb 2027, doubled entry thresholds — same OutlierKit/AIR coverage
- Meta: since July 2025, accounts repeatedly posting unoriginal content lose monetization and get reduced distribution; AI use is not banned, originality is the test — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/?p=187296); [CineRads](https://www.cinerads.com/blog/facebook-ai-content-monetization-policy) (commercial blog)
- TikTok Creator Rewards: one source says fully AI-generated content is explicitly excluded; another says it is eligible with labeling but earns little — conflicting — [Storrito](https://storrito.com/resources/what-tiktoks-ai-monetization-restrictions-signal-for-creator-income/); [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/?p=109419)

### Inferences
- Risk factors for an avatar channel: identical template/format across videos, no distinct per-video narrative, stock-only visuals, sensitive-topic advice from an AI "expert". Mitigation per the policy text: real scripts with original point of view, varied structure, visible human creative input.

### Gaps
- Official TikTok Creator Rewards eligibility text on AI content.
- Official Meta monetization policy text (only press coverage found).
- Whether 2026 YPP changes (Feb 2027) are real.

## 3. YouTube likeness protection (relevant to protecting own face)

### Cited Findings
- YouTube's likeness detection tool (in Studio's content detection area, works like Content ID for faces) expanded in May 2026 to all eligible users over 18; setup needs selfie plus ID verification; users can request removals — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/05/18/youtube-generative-ai-deepfake-likeness-detection-rollout/); [Exchange4Media](https://www.exchange4media.com/digital-news/youtube-expands-ai-likeness-detection-to-all-adult-users-amid-rising-deepfake-concerns-154745.html)
- Covers faces; one source (ETV Bharat) claims voice too, others do not — treat as UNVERIFIED — [ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/technology/youtube-expands-ai-likeness-detection-tool-to-all-users-over-18-enn26051803015)
- YouTube privacy-request process lets individuals request removal of AI content simulating their face or voice — [Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/apps-software/youtube-ai-synthetic-content-disclosure)

### Gaps
- Voice coverage of likeness detection unconfirmed.

## 4. Legal

### Takeaway
EU AI Act Article 50 transparency duties applied from 2 August 2026: deployers must visibly disclose deepfakes (a provider's machine-readable mark does not replace that). Machine-readable marking for pre-existing generators slipped to 2 Dec 2026. A creator publishing to EU audiences is a "deployer". In the US there is no federal synthetic-media label law (NO FAKES stalled); state laws (California, New York, Tennessee) and FTC fake-testimonial rules matter. India has binding labeling rules since Feb 2026.

### Cited Findings
- EU AI Act Art. 50 transparency obligations apply from 2 Aug 2026; Digital Omnibus did not delay deepfake labeling; only Art. 50(2) machine-readable marking got a grace period to 2 Dec 2026 for generative systems already on the market before 2 Aug 2026 — [CSA research note](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-eu-ai-act-article-50-transparency-20260729/); [ICTRecht](https://www.ictrecht.nl/en/blog/heads-up-ai-act-transparency-obligations-now-in-effect); [Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/viewpoints/2026/08/102nfqm/you-talkin-to-me-operationalising-the-eu-ai-acts-transparency-obligations)
- Art. 50(4): deployers must visibly disclose deepfakes; provider's machine-readable mark does not satisfy that duty — [McCann FitzGerald](https://www.mccannfitzgerald.com/knowledge/data-privacy-and-cyber-risk/ai-transparency-european-commissions-guidelines-on-article-50-part-2-deployer-obligations)
- Evidently artistic, satirical or fictional works get a lighter regime (disclosure that doesn't impede enjoyment); content already circulating before 2 Aug 2026 need not be relabeled — [William Fry](https://www.williamfry.com/?p=161081)
- Penalty for Art. 50 breaches: up to EUR 15m or 3% of worldwide turnover (Art. 99(4)) — [ICTRecht / explainer via search summary](https://www.ictrecht.nl/en/blog/heads-up-ai-act-transparency-obligations-now-in-effect)
- Commission draft Art. 50 guidelines published 8 May 2026; a voluntary Code of Practice on marking/labelling exists (~190 signatories by end July 2026); industry groups criticised drafts 3 July 2026; final-guideline status (reported 20 July) not confirmed — [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/?p=135293); [Learnsignal](https://www.learnsignal.com/blog/eu-ai-act-article-50-transparency-labelling-obligations-explained/); [BSA](https://www.bsa.org/files/policy-filings/07032026jointart50cop.pdf)
- Whether a first-person synthetic presenter of oneself counts as a "deepfake" (content resembling existing persons that would falsely appear authentic) is not settled in the sources I found.
- California AI Transparency Act (SB 942 as amended by AB 853): operative 2 Aug 2026; duties fall on large GenAI providers (>1m monthly CA users): free detection tool and provenance (latent) disclosures for image/video/audio; hosting-platform duties from 1 Jan 2027 — [Orrick](https://infobytes.orrick.com/2025-10-17/california-delays-its-ai-transparency-act-and-passes-new-content-laws/); [NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/californias-ongoing-ai-regulation-key-deadlines-arriving-2026-and-beyond)
- New York synthetic performer ad law (GBL 396-b): ads must disclose a fully simulated human who is not recognizable as a real person; effective 9 June 2026; $1,000 first / $5,000 later violations; applies to ads reaching NY audiences; platforms hosting ads exempt — [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-29-new-york-enacts-synthetic-performer-disclosure-law-for-advertisements-including-those-using-generative-ai); [Manatt](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/client-alert/new-york-synthetic-performer-law-what-advertisers-need-to-know)
- Federal NO FAKES Act reintroduced May 2026 (S. 4591 / H.R. 8915), cleared Senate Judiciary in June 2026, but stalled in October 2026 after Sen. Cruz objected to unanimous consent — [Manatt](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/client-alert/congress-reintroduces-the-no-fakes-act-what-s-new-in-the-2026-bill); [Blackburn](https://www.blackburn.senate.gov/2026/6/blackburn-coons-bipartisan-bill-to-protect-individuals-and-creators-from-deepfakes-passes-senate-judiciary-committee); [Daily Journal](https://www.dailyjournal.com/article/395055-no-fakes-act-dead-for-now-as-cruz-objects-to-unanimous-consent-request)
- Tennessee ELVIS Act (2024) is a state voice/likeness protection law, first of its kind — [Grammy.com](https://www.grammy.com/advocacy/issues-policy/no-fakes-act/)
- FTC Rule on fake reviews and testimonials (16 CFR Part 465, effective 21 Oct 2024): bans testimonials attributed to non-existent persons or people without real experience, including AI-generated; no AI-specific label rule — an avatar posing as a real customer falls under the general ban — [Sidley](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2024/08/30/u-s-ftcs-new-rule-on-fake-and-ai-generated-reviews-and-social-media-bots/); [Mintz](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/2191/2024-08-21-ftc-cracks-down-ai-generated-fake-reviews-ai-washington)
- India: IT Rules amendments on synthetically generated information notified 10 Feb 2026, effective 20 Feb 2026: platforms must label synthetic audio/visual content, embed provenance metadata where feasible, not strip identifiers; significant platforms collect user declarations; 3-hour takedown for flagged unlawful content. April 2026 draft would require continuous label display; finalization unconfirmed — [S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/india-tightens-oversight-on-ai-generated-content-under-it-rules/); [Gala Law](https://blog.galalaw.com/post/102micu/indias-2026-it-rules-amendment-regulating-ai-generated-content-and-accelerating)
- UAE: no AI-labeling statute found; cybercrime law (Federal Decree-Law 34 of 2021; one source cites 31 of 2021 — conflict) is the main tool; authorities issued deepfake warnings — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/gn-focus/company-news/uae-steps-up-fight-against-misinformation-as-digital-content-gets-harder-to-verify-1.500678158); [Facia](https://facia.ai/deepfake-law-directory/federal-decree-law-no-31-of-2021-on-cybercrimes-deepfake-law-most-important-provisions-and-sanctions/)
- Pakistan: one undated academic piece says laws are outdated and inadequate for deepfakes; no 2026 labeling law found — [LUMS](https://sahsol.lums.edu.pk/node/27600)

### Inferences
- Own likeness and own voice: right-of-publicity and impersonation laws are not engaged by definition; the live exposure is disclosure rules (EU Art. 50, India platform rules) and advertising/testimonial rules. Using someone else's face or voice needs their consent (all vendors require it, section 5) and engages publicity/privacy/impersonation law in many jurisdictions.
- If the avatar is a made-up synthetic person (not own likeness) used in ads, NY synthetic performer disclosure may apply to NY audiences.

### Gaps
- Right-of-publicity basics from a primary source (not fetched); state-by-state coverage (California AB 2602, others) not found.
- Official EU final guidelines text; official MeitY gazette text; UAE/Pakistan specific synthetic-media rules.

## 5. Vendor terms: consent, verification, commercial use

### Takeaway
All three vendors put consent responsibility on the user via warranty; Synthesia and HeyGen use a verification/consent recording for personal avatars; ElevenLabs Professional Voice Cloning uses a voice-match verification. Commercial use generally needs a paid plan.

### Cited Findings
- ElevenLabs Terms: voice recordings must be of your own voice or one you are "authorized to share"; you warrant no rights violated; free = non-commercial only, paid = commercial permitted; subject to Prohibited Use Policy; perpetual, irrevocable licence to your content incl. voice to provide/improve services, with opt-out of training in "Data use" settings; ElevenLabs "will not commercialize your voice on a standalone basis without your permission" — [ElevenLabs Terms](https://elevenlabs.io/terms-of-use)
- ElevenLabs Professional Voice Cloning verification: read a text prompt aloud, matched against the uploaded training voice; four retries then manual verification; misuse without consent can lead to permanent bans — [ElevenLabs voice guide](https://elevenlabs.io/voice-guide)
- HeyGen Terms: paid plans (Creator, Pro, Business) own inputs/outputs and may use outputs commercially; free-plan outputs are personal/non-commercial/evaluation only; broad licence to your content incl. model training; prohibits uploading images of individuals without consent and impersonation, and political campaigning content — [HeyGen Terms](https://www.heygen.com/terms)
- HeyGen processes biometric data during avatar creation with explicit consent and uses it to confirm identity and prevent misuse — [HeyGen Policy](https://www.heygen.com/policy)
- Synthesia: personal avatar requires a second consent video and agreement to use video/audio for authentication; no avatar made from anyone's likeness without explicit documented consent; customer must obtain rights from anyone whose likeness is used for custom avatars; customer retains ownership of videos; deletion requests can take up to 90 days — [Synthesia AI governance](https://www.synthesia.io/ai-governance); [Synthesia User Terms](https://www.synthesia.io/legal/user-terms-of-service)

### Gaps
- Watermark rules per plan for HeyGen/Synthesia not found; Synthesia full commercial-use clause not read; ElevenLabs Prohibited Use Policy content not read.

## 6. Safety: watermarking, C2PA, protecting your own clone

### Cited Findings
- C2PA metadata is read by YouTube (auto-label) and LinkedIn (tag); metadata is easily lost on re-encoding and platforms admit they cannot catch everything — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/396225/)
- Regulatory push for provenance: EU Art. 50(2) machine-readable marking (providers; to 2 Dec 2026 for legacy generators), California SB 942/AB 853 latent disclosures, India rules on provenance metadata — see section 4 citations
- Protecting yourself: YouTube likeness detection (selfie + ID); YouTube privacy removal requests; ElevenLabs voice stays exclusive to your account by default and you can opt out of training/delete voice models; Synthesia avatar deletion on request — see sections 3 and 5

### Inferences
- Practical measures consistent with sources: keep consent recordings, keep voice/avatar private, use verification-gated vendors, enable training opt-outs, retain C2PA metadata where tools support it.

### Gaps
- Voice-specific detection/takedown tools for own cloned voice on social platforms: not found.
- HeyGen/Synthesia/ElevenLabs outputs' watermark/C2PA support: not confirmed.
